Ask about a count

Based in Atlanta. Working with tree nurseries in Georgia.

What you get

After one pass through your field, you get three things.

A count you can check

We count the trees in each block and each row. You get the total, and you get the number by species.

A map on your phone

Open a link on your phone and see your own field. Tap a block to see what is in it. No app to install and no training needed.

A record for every tree

Every tree gets its own ID and its own line in a spreadsheet, so your office and your sales team work from the same numbers.

You do not buy equipment, you do not learn software, and nobody has to walk the rows with a clipboard.

We fit the work to your farm. If you only need one block counted, or you need it organized by your own block and row names, that is what we do.

How it works

1. We walk your farm with you

We come out once, look at your blocks, and agree on what to count and when.

2. We collect it

We cover your rows the way that suits the field, with a drone from the air or on foot. We stay out of your way and we do not need your crew.

3. You get your map

We send you a link and a spreadsheet. If the numbers do not look right to you, tell us and we go back out.

Who this is for

Field grown tree nurseries. If you have blocks of trees in the ground, several species, and a count that lives on paper or in one person's head, this is built for you.

It is most useful before a selling season, when the sales team needs to know what is actually out there, and before or after a dig, when the count changes fast.

What we are building next

Count, height, and caliper. Three parts of one record for every tree, with its own ID.

Counting is the first step. We are also building a way to measure tree height and trunk caliper, using a small ground robot that drives the rows and measures with a laser sensor. That work is in testing and it is not for sale yet. Every tree on your map gets its own ID, so the same tree can be found again on the next flight and its record follows it from the field to the sale. If height and caliper are what you care about most, tell us, because that is how we decide what to build first.

Who is behind this

Collective Robotics is led by Lucas Trujillo, a robotics engineer and computer science graduate student in Atlanta who builds the flight and measurement systems himself. He spends his time on farms and in the field, because a number that does not match what a grower sees walking the rows is not worth sending.
